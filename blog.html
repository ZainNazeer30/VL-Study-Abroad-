Study Guide · Italy

How to Apply to Universities in Italy

A step-by-step overview for international students

Italy pairs centuries of academic tradition with some of the most affordable public universities in Europe, and a growing list of degrees taught entirely in English. Here's the path most students follow.

1. Choose your course and university

Start by shortlisting programmes that match your background and budget. Public universities such as the University of Bologna, Padua, Sapienza (Rome), and the Politecnicos in Milan and Turin all offer strong international programmes. Check the language of instruction, entry requirements, and tuition (which is often income-based at public universities).

2. Prepare your documents

Typical requirements include your academic transcripts and degree/diploma, a valid passport, a motivation letter, and English proficiency (IELTS/TOEFL) if the course is in English. Some programmes ask for a CV or portfolio.

3. Apply through Universitaly and the university portal

International students pre-enrol through the Universitaly portal and, in parallel, apply on the university's own admissions system. Deadlines vary by university and intake, so confirm each one early.

4. Value evaluation and visa

Once admitted, your qualifications are validated (a "Dichiarazione di Valore" or a CIMEA statement), and you apply for a type-D study visa at the Italian embassy. Prepare proof of accommodation, funds, and insurance.

Visa Guide · France

France Student Visa: A Simple Walkthrough

The documents, the Campus France step, and the interview

France offers world-class universities at very affordable public tuition, and the student visa process is straightforward once you know the order of steps.

1. Get your admission

Secure an offer from a recognised French institution. English-taught programmes are widely available at universities and grandes écoles.

2. Complete the Campus France (Études en France) procedure

Most students must go through Campus France before the visa. You create an account, build your file, list your programme, and attend a short academic interview. This step connects your admission to the visa application.

3. Apply for the long-stay student visa (VLS-TS)

After Campus France validates your file, book your visa appointment. Core documents usually include your admission letter, passport, proof of funds (around the required monthly amount), accommodation proof, and insurance.

4. After you arrive

Validate your VLS-TS online within the first months of arrival so it acts as your residence permit for the year.

Funding · France & Italy

Top Scholarships for France & Italy

Ways to make studying abroad more affordable

Studying in Europe is already cost-effective at public universities, and scholarships can reduce it further. Here are the main routes to explore.

France

  • France Excellence Eiffel Scholarship — a prestigious government scholarship for master's and PhD students.
  • Émile Boutmy Scholarship — for international students admitted to Sciences Po.
  • University & IDEX scholarships — many universities (Paris-Saclay, Grenoble Alpes, Côte d'Azur) offer their own merit awards.

Italy

  • DSU regional right-to-study grants — need-based awards that can cover fees plus a living stipend.
  • University merit scholarships — e.g. Politecnico di Milano, Bologna Study Grants, Padua Excellence Scholarships.
  • Invest Your Talent in Italy — a government programme for selected master's fields.

Deadlines and eligibility change every cycle, so always confirm on the official pages, and apply early.

Admissions · France & Italy

Can You Study in France or Italy Without IELTS?

What your options really are

"Can I study abroad without IELTS?" is one of the most common questions we hear. The honest answer: sometimes yes, but it depends on the university and the course. Here's how it actually works.

1. Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter

If your previous education was taught in English, some universities accept a Medium of Instruction letter from your school or college instead of IELTS. Not every university allows this, so it must be confirmed programme by programme.

2. English-taught courses may still ask for a test

Many English-taught degrees in France and Italy do require IELTS, TOEFL, or an equivalent, because it is the clearest proof of your English level for admissions and the visa. Where a test is required, IELTS is usually the safest choice.

3. Online interviews and university tests

Some universities assess English through their own online test or a short interview instead of IELTS. Others accept alternatives such as Duolingo English Test or PTE.

4. Studying in French or Italian

If you plan to study in French or Italian, you'll typically need proof of that language (e.g. DELF/DALF or CILS) rather than English.

Costs · France & Italy

The Real Cost of Studying in France & Italy

Tuition, living, and how to keep it affordable

One of the biggest reasons students choose France and Italy is value: public universities are far cheaper than the UK, US, or Australia, and scholarships can lower the cost further. Here's a realistic picture (always confirm current figures on official pages).

Tuition (public universities)

  • France — roughly €170 to €3,770 per year depending on the level and university; many institutions charge the lower rate to international students.
  • Italy — tuition is income-based, commonly around €0 to €4,000 per year, and fee waivers are widely available for students from lower-income families.

Living costs

Budget roughly €700 to €1,200 per month for accommodation, food, and transport. Paris and larger cities sit at the higher end; smaller student cities are cheaper.

Proof of funds for the visa

Both countries ask you to show you can support yourself for the year as part of the student visa. The exact amount changes each year, so confirm the current figure before you apply.

Ways to reduce the cost

  • Apply for scholarships (Eiffel, Émile Boutmy, DSU regional grants, university awards).
  • Choose affordable student cities and university housing.
  • Part-time work is permitted within the limits your student visa allows.
